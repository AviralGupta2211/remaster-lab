AI stem split

HTDemucs · Meta AI · MIT

Separates vocals, drums, bass and everything else with the HTDemucs neural network, running on this computer. Nothing is uploaded. The first run downloads the model (about 112 MB); after that it is cached in this browser.

Use a separation server instead

If you run the Remaster Lab server (see the project README), it does the same split in under a minute on a GPU. Pages opened on claude.ai cannot reach outside servers, so this works when you run your own copy of the app.

Time runs left to right, pitch runs bottom (30 Hz) to top (16 kHz). Brightness is loudness over an 80 dB range. Kick drums sit at the bottom, hi-hats at the top, voices as stacked horizontal lines in between.

Energy in six bands across the song, in dB. Where a line drops, that range thins out; that is where EQ moves matter most.

Self-similarity: each pixel compares two moments. Bright squares on the diagonal are sections; bright off-diagonal blocks are repeats.

Load a song to see its equation.

The 93 ms around the playhead, written as a sum of sine waves (amplitude · sin(2π · frequency · t + phase), t in seconds from the playhead). Grey is the real waveform; the coloured line is the equation. More terms, closer fit.

The constellation map: the loudest peaks in a 6 s window around the playhead. Each anchor peak is paired with up to 6 later peaks; each pair (frequency 1, frequency 2, time gap) becomes one 24-bit hash. Lines show pairs from a few anchors.
